What Information Should I Leave for a Babysitter?

At minimum, a babysitter needs emergency contacts (you, a backup adult, and the pediatrician), any allergies or medications and how to handle them, the house and wifi basics, bedtime and routine notes, and a clear way to reach you. A fixed printed list covers one evening well, but it goes stale the moment plans change — a later pickup, a new allergy, a different bedtime — which is the main reason people search for something better than a printable template. This is a logistics checklist, not medical or safety advice; use your own judgment and any relevant emergency guidance alongside it.

Last reviewed 2026-09-15

The core information every sitter needs

  • Emergency contacts: you, a backup adult who can respond quickly, and the pediatrician's number.
  • Allergies and medications: what they are, what to do if a reaction happens, and any medication timing.
  • House basics: wifi password, alarm code, where the first-aid kit is, and any doors or areas that are off-limits.
  • Bedtime and routine: the actual sequence a child expects, not just a bedtime, since routine deviations are often what cause an evening to go sideways.
  • How and when to reach you: your expected return time and how you'd like to be contacted for something non-urgent versus urgent.

Why printable templates go stale

Search results for this topic are dominated by printable templates — titles like "Free Printable Babysitter's Information Guide," "Babysitting Checklist (Free Printable)," and "The Ultimate Grandparents' Emergency Babysitting Checklist" recur across many sites, which shows how strongly people want a fill-in-the-blank sheet rather than a philosophy of what to include. The problem with a printed sheet is that it's accurate exactly once: the day it's printed. A new allergy, a changed pickup time, or a different emergency contact means either re-printing or, more often, telling the sitter verbally and hoping it's remembered.

Making it something the sitter can actually check mid-visit

The fix isn't a better template, it's a living version of the same information that updates the moment something changes and that the sitter can check on their own without a phone call. That means the sitter needs access to current information without necessarily needing to install an app themselves — a share link they can open once is a lower-friction ask than an account signup for someone helping out for one evening.

How grandparents fit in differently than a paid sitter

A grandparent or regular family helper covering care is a different case than a one-off paid sitter: they're likely to help repeatedly, so a durable, always-current version of the same information is worth more to them than a one-time printout, and they may already know the house and routine well enough that only the changed details (a new allergy, a schedule shift) actually need flagging each time. The search pattern around this topic includes dedicated grandparent-specific checklists for exactly this reason — a distinct document called out separately from the general babysitter sheet, because a grandparent covering a weekend is doing something closer to running the household than watching it for three hours.

Frequently asked questions

What should be on a babysitter information sheet?

Emergency contacts, allergies and medications with instructions, house basics like wifi and alarm codes, the actual bedtime routine, and how to reach you. Treat it as a logistics checklist, not a substitute for your own judgment or emergency guidance.

What do I need to tell a new babysitter before they arrive?

Beyond the written sheet, walk through anything that's easy to miss in writing: where things physically are (first-aid kit, flashlight, fuse box), any household quirks, and what counts as an emergency worth calling you immediately about versus waiting.

Is a printable babysitter sheet good enough?

For a single evening with no last-minute changes, yes. The gap shows up when something changes after it's printed — a new allergy, a later pickup — since the sitter is then working from outdated information unless someone remembers to tell them verbally.
