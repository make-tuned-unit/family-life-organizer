Best Cozi Alternatives (2026)

People search for a Cozi alternative when the free tier that used to feel complete starts to feel like a trial: reviewers describe a 30-day calendar view limit, ads, and login or widget failures on an app some have used for ten years. The strongest alternatives are apps whose free tier is not a time-limited preview of the real product. Cozi's own reviewers also credit it with a decade of quiet reliability before this shift, which is worth weighing before switching. Key takeaway: Cozi's paywall has crept onto features that used to feel free; the strongest alternatives don't time-limit the free tier at all.

Last reviewed 2026-09-15

Why people search for a Cozi alternative

Cozi is the best-known free family calendar and list app, and for years it earned real loyalty: "I have used this app for probably 10 years" — an App Store reviewer, 2★, "Used to be great," App Store US, 2026-07-31. That loyalty is what makes the recent complaints land hard. Reviewers describe the free tier narrowing: "Charges you to plan 30 days in advance" — an App Store reviewer, 1★, "Bad," App Store US, 2026-08-16. Others cite a steep subscription: "I was really disappointed with this app. Even after paying for the Gold subscription at $30/yr, most features were still locked and only available at the $80/yr subscription" — an App Store reviewer, 2★, "Requires $80/yr subscription to use," App Store US, 2026-08-24.

Technical friction compounds it: "The widget always says I'm signed out even though I'm not" — an App Store reviewer, 1★, "Widget Doesn't Work," App Store US, 2026-08-09. And a growing AI push is unwelcome to long-tenure users: "It seems like all the update are pushing more AI into this app" — an App Store reviewer, 2★, "Too many changes," App Store US, 2026-09-11.

What Cozi does well (ten-year loyalty is real)

Cozi's core product — a shared calendar with per-person color coding and grocery/to-do lists — is simple enough that whole families, including multi-generational households, still rely on it daily. Reviewers who have used it for six or ten years are describing a genuinely dependable default, not a fad. Its meal planner and recipe box add real value on top of scheduling. Anyone frustrated by the newer paywalls and ads is reacting to recent changes layered on a product that, at its base, still works as advertised for a lot of households.

Ranked alternatives

The complaints driving people away from Cozi are about the free tier narrowing over time — a calendar view cap, ads, a subscription that used to feel optional. So this ranking weighs, in order: whether the core features stay free with no time limit, whether the app is ad-free without paying, and how broad the free feature set is beyond a calendar and a list.

  • TimeTree. Free and ad-supported (ad removal is paid), with useful per-event chat. A concentrated group of its own reviewers are currently leaving over AI features that reportedly train on calendar data even after opting out — worth knowing before you land there instead.
  • FamilyWall. A broader free-first hub (calendar, tasks, photos, location, finance) praised for elder-care coordination, but its own reviewers report frequent crashes after updates and broken invite flows that block new members from joining.
  • Skylight Calendar. An always-on physical wall display, not a phone app, so it solves a different problem: ambient visibility without ads or a login. It costs $170–$630 in hardware plus an optional roughly $79-a-year subscription for meal planning and chore rewards.
  • Maple. An AI-forward family organizer with an email-to-task assistant and chore tracker. Its own users report the same pattern driving people off Cozi: "recent updates introducing subscription models for previously free features" (paraphrased via aggregated user reports).
  • Kinrows. A free-core household organizer covering calendar, lists, budget, pantry, chores, trips and more, with no ads, no data sale, and no time-limited calendar view; only the optional AI Concierge is paid. It is iPhone-only and launches September 2026, so it is not an option today or for a mixed-OS household.

Who should stay with Cozi

If your household has used Cozi for years without hitting the 30-day view cap or minding the ads, and the current feature set still covers what you need, switching costs more than it saves. Cozi's cross-platform reach (iOS, Android, web), meal planner, and long track record are real strengths that a newer app has to earn from zero. Cozi Gold is listed at $39 on Cozi's own site (cozi.com/cozi-gold, checked 2026-09-15), though the page does not state whether that is a monthly or yearly price — a reviewer separately cited $80 a year, possibly an older or bundled tier — so confirm the current terms at checkout before assuming either figure.

Frequently asked questions

Is Cozi Gold worth it?

Cozi's own site lists Cozi Gold at $39, though the page does not state whether that is a monthly or yearly price (cozi.com/cozi-gold, checked 2026-09-15). An App Store reviewer separately cited an $80-a-year figure, possibly an older or bundled tier; check the exact current terms at checkout. Gold removes ads, unlocks month view and calendar search on mobile, adds up to three reminders per event, and adds a birthday tracker and shopping mode. Whether it's worth it depends on how much the free tier's 30-day view cap and ads bother you.

Why does Cozi look different on Android than iPhone?

One reviewer described Cozi as looking "great on apple, terrible on pixel" (2★, App Store US, 2026-08-24), suggesting the iOS and Android apps do not currently match in design or reliability. This isn't a gap Kinrows can close either, since Kinrows is iPhone-only with no Android app; a mixed-OS household bothered by this specific complaint should look for a cross-platform app built to the same standard on both operating systems.

What's a free alternative to Cozi?

Kinrows' core app — calendar, lists, budget, pantry, chores and more — is free for the entire household with no ads and no time-limited calendar view, funded by a separate, optional Concierge AI subscription. TimeTree and Google Calendar's sharing feature are also free (TimeTree's free tier shows ads; Google Calendar's does not). Kinrows is iPhone-only and does not launch until September 2026.

Why did Cozi start pushing AI features?

A cluster of long-tenure Cozi reviewers say recent updates keep adding AI features they did not ask for: "It seems like all the update are pushing more AI into this app" (2★, App Store US, 2026-09-11). Cozi has not published a specific rationale that this page could verify. Kinrows takes the opposite approach: its AI Concierge is a separate, optional, paid add-on never bundled into the free core experience, so using the free app never means encountering AI features you didn't choose.
