# Hearth Display — Complaint Research

## Overview

Hearth Display (Hearth Display Inc.) is a physical wall-mounted family calendar tablet/display plus a "Hearth Companion" mobile app (App Store id 6498717775, syncing calendars, routines, and an SMS AI assistant called "Hearth Helper"). Pricing found in reviews: hardware around $700, plus a subscription reported at roughly $84/year (~$9/month) required to unlock "basic stuff like to-dos" after the trial. Strengths cited by users: consolidates whiteboards/multiple calendars into one visible household hub; AI photo-import of events praised as a time-saver; effective at reducing kids' nagging when task lists work.

Hearth is a useful contrast case for Kinrows because it is hardware-anchored (large upfront cost) and paywalls basic organizer functions (to-dos) behind an ongoing subscription — versus Kinrows' free core app with an optional AI add-on.

## Complaint clusters

### 1. Expensive hardware that becomes a "paperweight"/"picture frame" without ongoing subscription
- **Problem:** Core household-management functions (to-do lists) stop working once the subscription lapses, after a $700 hardware purchase.
- **Quotes (App Store US RSS, https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=6498717775/sortBy=mostRecent/page=1/json, accessed 2026-09-15):**
  - "We have owned the hearth for a year now, and truly have enjoyed it. Our subscription is up in September so we decided to cancel it since it's 84$ a year. In this economy we just don't have that. Because of canceling we cannot do any of the basic stuff. Like the to dos. For the price of it and the lack of 'perks' I can honestly say I regret buying this. Such a waste of money and scam." — Aspencupcake, 1★, "Overly expensive," 2025-08-25
  - "I had such high hopes for this item. And like many others it's the most expensive frame that's just hung on our wall. It hasn't been used in almost a year. Without the premium subscription the only thing it does is display a calendar... All the premium features are just basic features you would think is part of the product." — KF2, 1★, "Expensive calendar display," 2025-08-18
  - "It seems odd that you pay a lot for the calendar that then wants you to subscribe to use its features. Seems like the price point of the calendar should come down significantly if they want to charge you monthly/annually just to use it." — JBur25, 2★, "Scam," 2026-06-17
  - (Reddit, paraphrased via uninfluencedreview.com, accessed 2026-09-15): premium model "just sits there. It's not useable."
- **User type:** Families who made a large one-time hardware purchase expecting it to be "done."
- **Intent:** Buyer's-remorse / value comparison.
- **Related Kinrows capability:** Free core app (calendar, lists, pantry, budget, etc.) with no hardware purchase required; Concierge is the only paid, optional layer, at $4.99–$9.99/month.
- **Candidate page type:** "Hearth Display alternatives" / "family command center without buying hardware" comparison page.
- **Conversion potential:** High — direct pricing-model contrast is a strong wedge.

### 2. Interface/app disconnect between the wall display and mobile companion app
- **Problem:** The wall unit and phone app feel like two different products that "do not speak to each other," requiring duplicate effort.
- **Quote:** "Both the interface itself on my big screen — what my husband calls 'the most expensive picture frame of all time'... and the 'companion app.' The interfaces do not speak to each other... This is a waste of $700." — thatmeag, 1★, "How have you not caught up to what other apps like this are doing in 2025?," 2025-08-14
- **User type:** Households actively trying to use both surfaces.
- **Intent:** Usability complaint.
- **Related Kinrows capability:** Single iPhone app as the one interface (no separate hardware/software split).
- **Candidate page type:** fold into hardware-vs-app comparison content.
- **Conversion potential:** Medium.

### 3. Missing basic features other calendar apps have (widgets, multiple alerts, location search, voice)
- **Problem:** Lacks iOS widgets, multiple alerts per event, robust location/business search, voice event creation, and smart-home integration (Alexa/Google Home).
- **Quotes:**
  - "The app works, but lacks a lot of the functionality you see in other calendar apps. For example, being able to set multiple alerts for the same event. The 'Add Location' option is also missing a lot of locations... What would make this app most useful for me is the addition of a widget." — Potatospam7, 3★, "Needs more functionality," 2026-03-05
  - "Features like widgets, smart voice event creation, Alex[a] skill enablement and google home compatibility are some features I would love to see incorporated in such a paid application. Hearth Companion App needs a search function for added events, tasks and appointments." — CL0V3, 3★, "App leaves desires," 2026-02-04
  - "This app is overall helpful in keeping our family organized... However it is not the greatest... a little clunky... I also wish it offered widgets." — katroot09, 3★, "Helpful family calendar," 2026-05-17
- **User type:** Power users comparing to free calendar apps.
- **Intent:** Feature-gap complaint.
- **Related Kinrows capability:** Not directly widget-verified; per-person color coding and two-way phone-calendar sync are relevant contrasts.
- **Candidate page type:** fold into general comparison content.
- **Conversion potential:** Low-medium.

### 4. Household adoption problem ("only works if everyone already buys in")
- **Problem:** Reddit commentary frames Hearth's value as entirely dependent on whether the whole household — especially a reluctant spouse — already uses digital calendars; the device itself can't fix non-adoption.
- **Quote (paraphrased via uninfluencedreview.com, accessed 2026-09-15):** "If your husband won't use a free shared calendar, why would he use the expensive shared calendar?" (cited as a highly-upvoted, 263-upvote Reddit comment).
- **User type:** The "carries the mental load, partner won't engage" persona — same core persona as the mental-load theme in questions-and-problems.md.
- **Intent:** Skeptical purchase research ("is Hearth Display worth it").
- **Related Kinrows capability:** none directly solves partner-adoption; relevant messaging angle: low-cost/no-hardware entry removes the financial risk of a partner not adopting it.
- **Candidate page type:** "Is a family command center worth it if my partner won't use a calendar" blog post.
- **Conversion potential:** Medium-high (speaks to root cause, not just tool choice).
