# SUMMARY-B — Top recurring complaint/problem clusters (ranked)

Sources: OurHome, AnyList, Maple, Hearth Display, Picniic, default tools (Google Calendar / Apple Reminders / group chats) and the question/problem phrasing research in this directory. Ranked by strength of evidence, directness of match to a Kinrows capability, and conversion potential. OurHome and Picniic are effectively defunct; their quotes are mostly paraphrased from secondary sources (flagged in each file). AnyList and Hearth Display quotes are verbatim App Store reviews with dates.

1. Real-time list/calendar sync silently breaking, causing duplicate work or missed events. "Purchased items are crossed off the list on one phone but still appear as unpurchased on the other." (AnyList, 2-star, 2026-07-25); echoed by Google Calendar ("can't see events I added") and Maple ("everything disappear[ed]").
2. Shared calendars/apps don't work if the partner won't buy in. "If your husband won't use a free shared calendar, why would he use the expensive shared calendar?" (Reddit via uninfluencedreview.com).
3. The mental load is invisible labour, not a missing-tool problem. "No calendar feature is going to cure a partner of [not sharing the load]." (sophiehines.substack.com).
4. Apps paywalling previously-free features after onboarding. Paraphrase: "Recent updates introducing subscription models for previously free features… significant user frustration." (Maple).
5. Expensive hardware/subscription bundles stripping basic functions on cancel. "Because of canceling we cannot do any of the basic stuff. Like the to dos… Such a waste of money and scam." (Hearth Display, 1-star, 2025-08-25).
6. Well-funded family apps quietly shutting down, stranding users. Paraphrase: "The app has been discontinued, and the site no longer works." (Picniic).
7. Abandonware, listed but unsupported. Paraphrase: "No updates recorded since 2022." (OurHome).
8. Family group chats becoming unmanageable noise burying logistics. Paraphrase: "40% of respondents indicated they were overwhelmed by group chat messages and notifications." (see default-tools.md for source).
9. No structured path from "said in the family chat" to "actually on the list/calendar" (inferred pattern, default-tools research).
10. Losing a specific integration (voice assistant, widget) that was the reason for choosing the app. "It has become useless in my house which is totally Alexa ecosystem… Hate to rate it this low but it is useless." (AnyList, 1-star, 2026-08-15).
11. "What to leave the babysitter/grandparent" served only by static printables, not a living shared tool. "Free Printable Babysitter's Information Guide" (pattern across 6+ sources).
12. Chores/allowance decisions searched by exact age band; apps answer with static price charts, not per-child guidance. "Should you tie allowances to chores" (Substack essay title).
13. Privacy/data-sale concerns as a late-funnel trust check. "Personal information is sold or rented to third parties, shared for third-party marketing, and personalized advertising is displayed." (Common Sense Privacy Evaluation, re: Cozi).
14. Confusing rebrands/duplicate app listings eroding trust. Two separate "OurHome" App Store listings, different developers/ratings.
15. Missing basic modern-app expectations (widgets, multi-alert, search) even in paid products. "Features like widgets, smart voice event creation, Alex[a] skill enablement and google home compatibility are some features I would love to see incorporated in such a paid application." (Hearth Companion, 3-star, 2026-02-04).

Strongest overall: #1, #2/#3 and #5 — verbatim evidence, root-cause rather than surface bug, and a clean mapping to Kinrows' free-core, private-by-design, single-app positioning.
